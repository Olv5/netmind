What is NetMind?
NetMind is a spaced repetition system built around active recall — you write answers, not just flip cards. It uses the SM-2 algorithm to schedule when each item comes back, so you study smarter, not harder. Items you struggle with come back sooner. Items you know well space out further.
Structure
Everything lives inside a Topic. Each topic has four folders:
Atomic
Single-fact questions. "What is a MAC address table?" — isolated recall, one correct answer.
Process
Step-by-step reasoning. "What happens when a switch receives a frame?" — trace the logic in order.
Problem
Applied scenario. "Host A sends a frame but the switch doesn't know B's MAC. What happens?" — apply knowledge to a novel situation.
Reconstruction
Full synthesis from scratch. "Reconstruct how a packet travels across networks." — the hardest type. You own the knowledge when you can rebuild it without notes.
How a Review Session Works
1. Write your answer — Before revealing anything, write out your answer in the text box. This is the most important step. The act of retrieving information is what builds memory.
2. Reveal reference — Compare your answer to the reference. Be honest with yourself.
3. Grade yourself — Four options, each showing how many sessions until you see this card again:
Again
You missed key points or blanked. Card comes back in 1 session — today if you keep reviewing.
Hard
Got there, but with significant effort. Smaller interval increase.
Good
Mostly correct. Standard interval growth. Use this most of the time.
Easy
Complete and confident. Larger interval jump. Only use if it was genuinely trivial.
Again cards — come back 10 cards later in the same session, marked with "· AGAIN" in the type label so you know it's a retry. If you hit Again on the retry too, it won't requeue a third time — it just moves to the next session to avoid loops.
SM-2 Scheduling
Every item has an interval (sessions until next review) and an ease score (starts at 2.5, ranges 1.2–4.0). When you grade a card:

Again → interval = 1 · ease −0.4
Hard → interval × 1.2 · ease −0.15
Good → interval × ease · no change
Easy → interval × ease × 1.3 · ease +0.2
A card you grade Good repeatedly: 1 → 2 → 5 → 12 → 30 sessions. The intervals compound — that's the point. Struggling cards stay close. Mastered cards drift far apart.
Daily New Card Limit
Set via ⚙ Daily new in the sidebar. Defaults to 20. This caps how many brand-new (never-reviewed) cards appear per day. Cards you've already reviewed always show up when due, regardless of this limit.

Without this limit, adding 100 cards at once means 100 new cards hit you immediately — overwhelming and counterproductive. The limit paces your learning. New cards queue up automatically for future days.
Other Controls
Skip ↷ Moves the card to the end of the current queue without grading it. Priority unchanged. Use when you want to come back to it later this session.
← Back Undoes the last action — grade or skip. Restores the card's previous review state in the database. You can go back multiple times.
⟳ Topic The small ⟳ next to each topic name starts a review scoped only to that topic. If nothing is due it asks if you want to review anyway.
↺ Reset Zeroes all review stats — sessions, streak, ease scores, intervals. Topics and items stay. Use when starting fresh.
Streak
Increments once per calendar day on your first graded card. Resets to 1 if you miss a day. Shows 🔥 at 2+ days. The streak only counts when you actually grade something — opening the app or skipping all cards doesn't count.
